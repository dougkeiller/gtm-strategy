# Evidence ladder

Every agent grades the evidence behind a claim by its **rung**, not by how many sources agree. Three articles repeating one press release are one source.

| Rung | What it is | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Synthetic: an AI persona, a model's estimate, the founder's belief | Synthetic buyer panel output; "we think owners will pay" |
| 1 | Opinion: what buyers say they think or would do | "I'd use that", survey intent, compliments |
| 2 | Past behaviour, described: what buyers say they actually did | "Last month we lost two claims because…" |
| 3 | Commitment of time or reputation | Pilot agreed, intro to a decision maker, repeat meeting |
| 4 | Commitment of money short of a full sale | LOI with price, deposit, paid pilot |
| 5 | Paying customer at a real price | Signed contract, invoice paid |

Third-party data (analyst reports, filings, benchmarks) is graded separately as HIGH (several independent primary sources), MEDIUM (one primary source) or LOW (inferred or secondary).

## Rules

1. Report the highest rung reached for each key hypothesis. A strategy whose beachhead rests on rungs 0–1 must say so in its first paragraph.
2. Interview counts are not evidence. Fifty opinions are still rung 1.
3. Price talk is directional. Willingness-to-pay answers (rung 1) give a range; only rungs 4–5 test the price.
4. Synthetic panel results are always rung 0. They generate hypotheses and interview questions; they never confirm anything.
5. When evidence conflicts, show both sides and say which you trust and why.

## Sources

- Pattyn, Dillen & Goetz (2026), "Validate Before You Build", *Computers* 15(8):535. In 16 Belgian software startups, the stronger performers all reached paying customers before the MVP, and the weaker ones clustered at informal discussion. The authors describe this as an exploratory association, not a causal finding.
- Fitzpatrick, *The Mom Test*: ask about past specifics, not future opinions, and count a meeting only by the commitment (time, reputation or cash) it produced.
- Ramanujam & Tacke, *Monetizing Innovation*: talk about price before building. This is stated-preference data and gives ranges only.
- Cordes (2026), Aalto University thesis: founders and investors *perceive* budget ownership, unsolicited inbound interest and independent accounts of one urgent problem as predictive of fit. These are beliefs, not tested predictors.
