# GTM frameworks the agents apply

Each line says what the source supports and how strong it is. Practitioner heuristics are labelled; use them as defaults to argue with, not laws.

## Beachhead and segment
- **One self-referencing beachhead** (Moore, *Crossing the Chasm*). Pick one segment whose members reference each other when buying, with a compelling reason to buy now, and deliver the *whole product* (everything, including partners, needed to fulfil that reason). Then expand to the adjacent segment ("bowling pin"). Reject plans that split effort across several segments. Built for disruptive tech sold to pragmatists; apply with judgement elsewhere.
- **Bottom-up beachhead sizing and a real persona** (Aulet, *Disciplined Entrepreneurship*). Size the beachhead as annual revenue at 100% share from account counts × price; name one real end user and ten lookalike customers; map the decision-making unit before choosing channels.
- **Repeatable case study** (Rob Snyder, practitioner). Describe the one buyer who says "hell yes": who, what they were trying to achieve, alternatives considered, why they bought, outcome. Snyder warns that personas and niches never bought anything. Reconciliation used here: the segment is the container, the case study is the unit of proof.

## Positioning
- **Competitive alternatives first** (Dunford, *Obviously Awesome*). Positioning has five linked parts: alternatives (including the status quo), unique capabilities, differentiated value, best-fit customers, market category. Capabilities are only differentiated relative to alternatives; position before writing the story.
- **Contextual differentiation** (Anthony Pierri, practitioner). With no direct competitor, answer "when would I use you?" by naming the trigger situation and the status-quo alternative, rather than forcing a feature comparison.

## Buyer and motion
- **Users and budget holders are separate columns** (Irina Haivas, Atomico; investor advice). Map each one's incentives. Test bottom-up and top-down routes and set the sequence explicitly, with the trigger at which the second motion starts.
- **Find the person whose problem the whole product solves** (Ev Kontsevoy, Teleport; one company's account). If users only use a slice of the product, the buyer may be someone else; changing buyer can change the message and the motion.
- **Personal sales before mass-media marketing** (Vomberg et al. 2026, *Journal of Marketing Research* 63(1); founder survey of 300+ B2B high-tech startups over 20 months). A larger budget share on personal selling did better early and worse later; mass-media marketing the reverse. Self-reported data.
- **Don't scale before the use case repeats** (Lee & Kim 2024, *Strategic Management Journal* 45(9); 38,000+ US startups). Startups that posted manager or sales jobs early were more likely to fail, with no offsetting benefit; observational. Treat the first sales hire as a scaling commitment that needs evidence of repeatability.
- **Founder-led until it is predictable** (several investors and operators, e.g. Anu Hariharan, YC Continuity). Practitioner consensus, not tested.

## First customers and price
- **Charge from the start** (Rob Walling, practitioner). Default to a discounted paid pilot; free pilots forfeit the payment test.
- **First ten sales as a fit test** (Rujul Zaparde, Zip; one founder). Zip closed its first ten customers cold, opening with advice conversations. A small Swedish case study (8 startups) found most first customers came from founders' networks. Pick one route and say why.
- **Talk price before building** (Ramanujam & Tacke, *Monetizing Innovation*). Establish value, then ask acceptable / expensive / too-expensive prices and per-feature value. Stated preference only: follow with a paid commitment.
- **Price conversations then a trial close** (Amit Bendov, Gong). About 50 mostly-cold calls on value, objections and price gave a floor; the paid trial close with beta users was the real test.

## Customer interviews
- **Past behaviour, not opinions** (Fitzpatrick, *The Mom Test*). Ask about specific past events; ban hypotheticals; score each conversation by the commitment of time, reputation or cash it produced.

## Moats
- **7 Powers** (Helmer). Scale economies, network economies, counter-positioning, switching costs, branding, cornered resource, process power.
