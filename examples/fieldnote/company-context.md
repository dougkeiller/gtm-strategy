# Company context: Fieldnote (fictional example)

Fieldnote is an invented company used to test this skill. Any resemblance to a real business is accidental.

## Company
- Name: Fieldnote. Founded 2026. Stage: pre-product (clickable prototype).
- Team: two founders (one ex-operations manager at a regional HVAC contractor, one engineer). The ops founder sells.
- Funding: USD 400k pre-seed; runway about 14 months.
- No parent company.

## Product
- Today (prototype): a phone app where field technicians dictate job notes; AI turns them into a structured job report, a parts list and a draft invoice line.
- Planned: integration with field-service management software; automatic warranty-claim drafting for equipment manufacturers.
- Pricing today: none.

## Customers and evidence
- Paying customers: none.
- Pilots: two HVAC contractors (8 and 35 technicians) have agreed to a free 60-day pilot starting next quarter.
- What buyers said: see `evidence-log.md`.

## Market and competition
- Assumed buyer: owner or operations manager at a residential HVAC/plumbing contractor with 10–100 technicians.
- Assumed user: field technician.
- Alternatives today: paper forms, typing notes into the field-service app at the end of the day, office staff calling technicians back.
- Known competitors: field-service management suites with note fields; general voice-to-text apps.

## Constraints
- No sales team; founder-led only for 12 months. US only.
- Leadership has ruled out building their own field-service management suite.

## Goal of this engagement
- Decide the beachhead segment, the first price, and a 90-day plan to turn the two pilots into paying customers.
